Eric's Video Ad Scripts

Ten hooks, six bodies, two closes. Any hook goes with any body and either close, so one session, about 45 minutes, turns into 120 new ads of about 45 seconds each. Every piece is written around what's in it for the homeowner.

📋 Read this first · 2 minutes

Your shoot setup

You've got the camera, the mic and the teleprompter, so this is mostly about framing and flow. Read everything top to bottom in one recording, and I'll cut it into 120 ads.

🔊 Audio Most important

  • You're mic'd on the camera. Perfect. Bad audio tanks an ad faster than anything, so do a 10-second test and play it back before you start.
  • Kill the noise. AC, fans, phones on silent. If desks are getting delivered Saturday, record before or after the crew is in the building.
  • A big empty office echoes. If it sounds hollow on playback, move closer to a wall with stuff on it, or put a rug down.

📐 Framing

  • Best: shoot vertical (9:16). Turn the camera sideways, frame yourself from mid-chest up, eyes in the upper third.
  • Shooting 4K landscape instead? That works too. Stand further back so you're framed from the knees up (3/4 body), centered, with some room above your head. That lets me crop it to vertical without cutting you off.
  • Camera at eye level, on a tripod, and don't move it between pieces. Same spot, same shirt, the whole session.

💡 Light and background

  • Light on your face, never a window behind you. Face the window or add a lamp in front of you.
  • Background: your office is great. A logo, a project photo or a clean shelf behind you beats a blank wall. Clear the clutter off the desk.

📜 Teleprompter

  • Paste the teleprompter text from the bottom of this page. It's in shoot order with every slate in it.
  • Keep the prompter right under or over the lens so your eyes stay on the camera.
  • Optional tip: if you ever get tired of thumb-scrolling, SpeakFlow is a teleprompter app that follows your voice and scrolls on its own. Totally optional. Your setup works fine.
The whole message in one line: Homeowners book when they know they're getting a fair price, who's actually building it, that it's backed by your 5-Star Lifetime Guarantee, and exactly what happens after they tap. Every ad ends by spelling out the next step, because that's the close that's booking best right now.

🎬 Record it in one take

  • One recording, top to bottom. Before each piece, say its name out loud ("Hook 3", "Body B", "CTA 2"), pause one second, then go.
  • Flubbed a line? Pause two seconds and start that sentence over. I keep the last clean take. No need to stop recording.
  • Need a break? Stopping between the hooks, the bodies and the CTAs is fine. Just keep the same spot and framing.
  • Record every piece: all 10 hooks (Hook 1 three times with the different towns), all 6 bodies (A to F), and both CTAs. Any hook goes with any body and either CTA, so I need them all.

✅ Check these before you record

  • "Some companies have twenty offices, fifty trucks and a hundred billboards" and "small team, our own crews, and we watch every dollar." Body A and ad copy B. These are your words from the original price video. Still true today? It also implies you don't run billboards, so tell us if you do.
  • "Same quality materials, no corners cut." Body A. The same aluminum and materials the bigger companies use. Say it only if that's how you'd put it.
  • "Family-owned." Ad copy A, B and C. The website says family-owned. Tell us if you'd rather say it another way.
  • Custom rafter brackets that raise the cover without touching the roof: is that a real Mr. Patio Cover feature? It's not in the script yet. Tell us and we'll add it.
  • "We come out and look at your space." Body C. Is the design meeting always at the customer's house? If some are by phone or video, we'll reword it.
  • "You get a real price in writing, no hidden extras, and no pressure to decide that day." Body C and ad copy C. Do they get the written price at the meeting, or a day or two later? If it's later, say "a real price in writing, soon after."
  • "Pick a time for your custom design meeting and free estimate." Both CTAs. Right now people answer a few questions, then pick a time on your calendar. If that ever changes, the CTA changes with it.
  • Hook 1: the towns. Take 1 = Corona, Chino, Rancho Cucamonga. Take 2 = Fontana, Riverside, Eastvale. Take 3 = Anaheim, Yorba Linda, Whittier. These are where most of your jobs are. Swap in any towns you want more work from.
  • No prices, and no years in business, on purpose. You said it best: nobody cares how long you've been in business, they care what's in it for them. So every piece opens on the customer. Prices come at the design meeting.

🎯 How to say it

  • Talk to one homeowner, not a crowd. Picture a customer sitting across your desk.
  • Hooks: word for word. I test them against each other, so the exact words matter. Start talking right away, no "hey guys".
  • Bodies: know the beats, say it your way. Short and sure of it, the way you'd say it on the phone.
  • Always finish the CTA with "Let's talk." The three steps are what get people to book, so say all three.

🥩 Bodies — "The Meat"

about 30 seconds each · record all 6

A is why you can price fairly. B is who they're hiring and your 5-Star Lifetime Guarantee. C walks them through the free custom design meeting. D is how you handle the permit, the HOA and the build. E is what other warranties leave out. F is getting their backyard back. Know the beats, then say it your way.

A
Fair price: no bloated overhead · 70 words
"Here's how patio cover pricing really works. Some companies have twenty offices, fifty trucks and a hundred billboards. Guess who pays for all that? You do. It's baked into your quote. We don't run it that way. Small team, our own crews, and we watch every dollar. Less overhead for us means a better price for you. Same quality materials, no corners cut. I'm Eric Engle, with Mr. Patio Cover."
B
Who you're hiring + the 5-Star Lifetime Guarantee · 67 words
"Here's who you're actually hiring. The crew that shows up at your house works for us. Our own guys, never subcontracted. That's why we can put our 5-Star Lifetime Guarantee on every patio cover we build. Leak-free for life, wind damage covered, workmanship for life, in writing. And if you ever sell your home, it transfers to the new owner. I'm Eric Engle, with Mr. Patio Cover."
C
The custom design meeting: what actually happens · 74 words
"Here's what happens at your free custom design meeting. We come out and look at your space. We talk about how you want to use it, how much shade, and the look you want. Replacing an old cover? Tearing it out and hauling it away is part of your quote. You get a real price in writing, no hidden extras, and no pressure to decide that day. I'm Eric Engle, with Mr. Patio Cover."
D
We handle everything: permit, HOA, build, clean-up · 73 words
"Here's the part nobody tells you about. A patio cover isn't just the build. It's the drawings, the city permit, sometimes the HOA. Most people end up chasing all that themselves. Not with us. We pull the permit and handle the HOA paperwork, and it's included. Then our own crew builds it, most in a day or two, and cleans up. One team, start to finish. I'm Eric Engle, with Mr. Patio Cover."
E
What other lifetime warranties leave out · 66 words
"Here's what most patio cover lifetime warranties leave out. Attach a cover to your house wrong, and water gets behind the stucco and rots the wall. Their warranty doesn't cover that. We attach it right the first time, and our 5-Star Lifetime Guarantee is in writing. Leak-free for life. If it ever leaks, we come back at our cost. I'm Eric Engle, with Mr. Patio Cover."
F
Get your backyard back · 70 words
"Picture this. It's July, it's a hundred and five, and your backyard is off-limits until sundown. Now picture walking out at two in the afternoon into real shade. Kids in the pool, grill going, friends over, nobody hiding inside. Most of our covers go up in a day or two. That's what a patio cover actually buys you. Your backyard back, all summer. I'm Eric Engle, with Mr. Patio Cover."

  • Shoot order: all hooks, then the bodies, then the CTAs, in one recording with slates.
  • Consistency: identical wardrobe/setting so any hook edits onto any body.
  • Delivery: talk to one person, not a camera. Authentic beats polished.
  • Naming: MPC_ERIC_V1_H03 · MPC_ERIC_V1_M01 · MPC_ERIC_V1_CTA01 → MPC_ERIC_V1_FIN_H03-M01-CTA01
  • Test plan: hooks get tested first, then bodies, then the two closes. We judge on booked design meetings, not clicks.
